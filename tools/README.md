# Tools and References

This guide separates force-field models, simulation engines, polymer-building workflows, and research papers. A package may automate a workflow, but it does not make the underlying model valid for every polymer: check each force field's chemical coverage and compare calculated properties with experiment or a trusted reference.

## Classical Force Fields and Model Representations

| Name | What it describes | Notes for polymer work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [AMBER](https://ambermd.org/) | A family of biomolecular force fields and simulation programs. The related GAFF extends AMBER-style parameters to common organic molecules. | GAFF: Wang, Wolf, Caldwell, Kollman & Case, “Development and testing of a general amber force field,” *Journal of Computational Chemistry* **25**(9), 1157–1174 (2004), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcc.20035). Do not treat protein/nucleic-acid parameter sets as general polymer parameters; see [AMBER documentation](https://ambermd.org/). |
| [CHARMM](https://academiccharmm.org/documentation) | A force-field family and simulation program, with parameter sets for biomolecules, lipids, and selected small molecules. | `CHARMM` can mean either the force-field family or the simulation engine. CGenFF: Vanommeslaeghe, Hatcher, Acharya et al., “CHARMM general force field: A force field for drug-like molecules compatible with the CHARMM all-atom additive biological force fields,” *Journal of Computational Chemistry* **31**(4), 671–690 (2010), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcc.21367). Check its chemical coverage before polymer use. |
| [OPLS-AA](https://doi.org/10.1021/ja9621760) | An all-atom potential developed for conformational energetics and organic-liquid properties. | Jorgensen, Maxwell & Tirado-Rives, “Development and testing of the OPLS all-atom force field on conformational energetics and properties of organic liquids,” *Journal of the American Chemical Society* **118**(45), 11225–11236 (1996), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/ja9621760). Validate torsions, charges, and condensed-phase properties for the polymer of interest. |
| [PCFF](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jpcb.2c01973) | A class-II, all-atom force-field family developed for synthetic polymers and related materials. The linked study applies PCFF to poly(epsilon-caprolactone); the [CFF93 polycarbonate paper](https://doi.org/10.1021/ja00086a030) is a related foundational source. | Confirm that the installed parameter set covers the chemistry and properties being studied. PCFF and COMPASS are related CFF-family models, but their parameters are not interchangeable. |
| [GROMOS](https://www.gromos.net/) | A biomolecular force-field family and simulation package; its parameter sets are tuned for particular molecular classes and condensed-phase data. | Representative force-field reference: Oostenbrink, Villa, Mark & van Gunsteren, “A biomolecular force field based on the free enthalpy of hydration and solvation: the GROMOS force-field parameter sets 53A5 and 53A6,” *Journal of Computational Chemistry* **25**(13), 1656–1676 (2004), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcc.20090). Not a universal synthetic-polymer force field. |
| [COMPASS](https://doi.org/10.1021/jp980939v) | An all-atom, class-II force field parameterized for condensed-phase properties of organic, inorganic, and polymeric materials. | Sun, “COMPASS: An ab initio force-field optimized for condensed-phase applications—overview with details on alkane and benzene compounds,” *The Journal of Physical Chemistry B* **102**(38), 7338–7364 (1998), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/jp980939v). Check installed version and parameter coverage. |
| [ReaxFF](https://doi.org/10.1038/npjcompumats.2015.11) | A bond-order reactive force field that can represent bond formation and breaking. | Foundational paper: van Duin, Dasgupta, Lorant & Goddard, “ReaxFF: A Reactive Force Field for Hydrocarbons,” *The Journal of Physical Chemistry A* **105**(41), 9396–9409 (2001), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/jp004368u). Validate the parameter set for the relevant elements, bonding, and reaction conditions. |
| [United-atom (UA)](https://trappe.oit.umn.edu/) | A representation that combines many carbon-bound hydrogens with the carbon into one interaction site; TraPPE-UA is one named parameter family. | UA is a level of representation, not a force field by itself. Polar hydrogens and heteroatoms may remain explicit, depending on the model. |
| [Coarse-grained (CG)](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.458541) | A representation that groups atoms into larger interaction sites. Kremer & Grest, “Dynamics of entangled linear polymer melts: A molecular-dynamics simulation,” *The Journal of Chemical Physics* **92**, 5057–5086 (1990), introduced a generic bead-spring polymer-melt model; [MARTINI 3](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-021-01098-3) is a chemistry-informed CG force field. | CG models trade chemical detail for larger length and time scales. State the mapping and target properties; a generic model is not automatically transferable to a specific polymer. |

## Notes on Scope

- A **force field** defines interactions; an **MD engine** integrates equations of motion; a **workflow package** automates some steps around an engine. These are related, but not interchangeable.
- UA and CG describe levels of molecular representation. TraPPE-UA, Kremer–Grest, and MARTINI are examples of specific parameterized models within those representations.
- When reporting software-based results, record the exact software release/commit, force-field family and parameter-set version, input structure, mapping, and validation protocol. A package or force-field family name alone is not enough to reproduce a calculation.
- For a new polymer chemistry, verify atom typing, charges, bonded parameters, non-bonded interactions, and any mapping choices. Report the validation data and simulation protocol with the results.
- Preprints, conference presentations, and peer-reviewed articles are labeled separately above. Recheck publication status when citing fast-moving 2026 work.
